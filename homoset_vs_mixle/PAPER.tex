\begin{abstract}
\noindent
The GuthrieSolv database is the largest literature compilation of experimental
small-molecule hydration free energies (\num{53895} records, \num{5309} molecules),
but it is heterogeneous and of uncertain quality: raw measurements are stored in
172 distinct unit strings spanning vapour pressures, Henry's-law constants,
aqueous solubilities and free energies, and it ships with no data-reconciliation
code. We (i) harmonize the convertible fraction of the database into Ben--Naim
hydration free energies (\num{16357} observations over \num{2675} molecules,
temperature-corrected, keyed by InChIKey), validating every conversion route
against the curated FreeSolv benchmark, and further recover much of the long tail of the 172
unit strings with an active-learning fixed-point loop that calibrates each unconverted unit against
already-converted anchors and admits it only when it passes the same Homoset noise gate
(\num{19} units, \num{+135} molecules, and \emph{improved} FreeSolv agreement); and (ii) use the resulting redundant,
multi-source, per-molecule observations to compare two families of reconciliation
method. The first is a \emph{proportional-similarity} (Homoset) gate --- a method originally devised in
the 1970s--1980s to standardize scattered human olfactory detection-threshold
data~\cite{devos1990} --- that aligns
whole measurement sources by a median offset and admits them only when the relative scatter of
the set falls below a noise level, governed by just two parameters (a noise level and a
dimension scale $L$); the
second is a \emph{mixle}-style hierarchical Gaussian partial-pooling model that
estimates a per-source bias, a per-source noise scale, and a shrunken per-molecule
posterior jointly by expectation--maximization, with an optional Student-$t$
robustification. On the 561-molecule FreeSolv overlap both methods outperform
naïve pooling. The Homoset gate is the strongest transparent \emph{curator}: when its noise scale
$L$ is anchored to the true experimental uncertainty it acts as a hard source-quality filter and,
on the well-sampled molecules, attains the best accuracy (\SI{0.088}{\kcal} at $\ge4$ observations)
while emitting a defensible curation flag. The hierarchical model is the strongest
\emph{reconciler}: it attains the lowest overall MAE and RMSE (\SI{0.249}{} and \SI{0.873}{\kcal}),
keeps all data, corrects per-source bias, and uniquely recovers sparse molecules the gate cannot
align. We argue the two are complementary --- reconcile with
the hierarchical model, triage with the Homoset gate --- and release the
harmonized dataset and code.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Experimental hydration (solvation-into-water) free energies underpin the
parameterization and validation of implicit-solvent models, physics-based free-energy
calculations, and machine-learned property predictors. The community gold standard,
FreeSolv~\cite{freesolv}, is small (\num{642} molecules) precisely because careful
curation is expensive. J.~P.~Guthrie assembled a far larger compilation from the primary
literature; after his death the family released it as GuthrieSolv~\cite{guthriesolv} with
explicit disclaimers about quality and an appeal for community curation. The database
mixes physical quantities and units within a single column, uses at least two hydration
standard states, and encodes data-trust flags in discrete uncertainty values. Crucially it
provides \emph{no} automated reconciliation --- its only quality-assurance code checks that
SMILES strings parse.

This is a natural test bed for multi-source data curation. Because many molecules appear in
GuthrieSolv multiple times --- across publications, temperatures and measurement techniques
--- each molecule carries a small, noisy, redundant set of observations once the raw values
are placed on a common scale. The curation question is then: given several disagreeing
measurements of the same quantity, what single value should one trust, and which molecules
should a human re-examine? We compare two answers with very different philosophies.

\section{Data harmonization}
\label{sec:harm}
GuthrieSolv stores a raw value (\texttt{value1}), a unit (\texttt{dimension1}), a temperature
(\texttt{te53p}) and, importantly, a measurement-type code (\texttt{process}) per record. The
process code is the key that disambiguates the 172 unit strings; Table~\ref{tab:process}
summarizes the convertible routes. The full per-unit conversion switchboard --- pressure units
to pascals, solubility units to \si{\mol\per\litre} via molecular weight, and the
pressure/concentration, concentration/pressure, mole-fraction, dimensionless-ratio and
Ostwald/Bunsen Henry conventions to \Kwa\ --- is implemented in \texttt{harmonize\_guthrie.py}.
We target the Ben--Naim standard state (transfer of one
mole per litre of ideal gas to one mole per litre of dilute aqueous solution), which is the
convention FreeSolv reports, so that converted values are directly comparable:
\begin{equation}
\dGhyd = -RT \ln \Kwa, \qquad \Kwa = C_{\mathrm{aq}}/C_{\mathrm{gas}} .
\label{eq:bennaim}
\end{equation}

Harmonization yields \num{16357} observations over \num{2675} molecules. We validated each route
by its per-observation residual against FreeSolv on the overlap, which exposed four systematic
errors that we corrected: (i) the mole-fraction Henry route was biased by exactly
$-RT\ln 10^3 = +\SI{4.09}{\kcal}$, a dropped litre-to-cubic-metre factor; (ii) the dimensionless
Henry ratios are stored with a process-dependent direction (the ``water/gas'' KWG rows hold the
air/water constant and must be inverted, the ``gas/water'' KGW rows hold \Kwa\ directly --- the
labels are effectively swapped in the source); (iii) a one-character regular-expression fault
silently dropped every logarithmic unit spelling (\texttt{log(M)}, \texttt{log(mol/L)},
\texttt{log(atm)}, \dots{}: several thousand rows) from the switchboard; and (iv) the mole-fraction
solubility spellings were unhandled. Repairing (iii)--(iv) --- pure deterministic conversions, not
model fits --- recovered \num{4330} rows and \num{202} molecules and, together with (i)--(ii),
took the per-molecule median agreement with FreeSolv to an MAE of \SI{0.299}{\kcal} ($R=0.965$),
from \SI{0.79}{\kcal} ($R=0.874$) at the outset. The direct free-energy records (FEOH, DGS) show
essentially zero median residual and constitute the clean reference; the Henry and pairing routes
are the noisier, conflict-generating sources.

\paragraph{What is and is not a unit conversion.} It is worth being explicit about the ceiling.
Of the \num{172} unit strings, only the Henry-constant and free-energy families are
\emph{unit conversions of} \dGhyd --- a molecule-independent monotone map --- and every one of
those is now converted. The remaining $\sim$\num{35000} rows are vapour pressures and aqueous
solubilities, which are \emph{different physical observables}: no molecule-independent function
maps a lone vapour pressure to a hydration free energy, because $\dGhyd$ depends on volatility and
solubility jointly ($\dGhyd = RT\ln[P_{\mathrm{vap}}/(RT\,S)]$). These are recovered only by
\emph{pairing} a vapour pressure with a solubility for the same molecule (\num{1722} molecules).
What then remains --- \num{750} vapour-pressure-only and \num{1666} solubility-only molecules --- is
genuinely unrecoverable: a single observable is a data gap that no amount of arithmetic can close.

\subsection{Active-learning calibration of the long-tail units}
\label{sec:activeloop}
The hand-coded switchboard covers the high-frequency units but leaves a long tail of the 172
strings unconverted. Rather than hand-code each remaining spelling, we recover many of them with
a retro-feedback loop that reuses the Homoset homogeneity gate (Eq.~\eqref{eq:ps}) as its
acceptance criterion. Let the \emph{anchors} be the molecules already assigned a $\dGhyd$. For
each unconverted (unit, process) group we fit the best monotone map
$\dGhyd \approx a\,f(\text{value}) + b$ with $f\in\{\log_{10},\,-\log_{10},\,\ln,\,\text{id}\}$
(a robust, trimmed least-squares fit) against the anchor values of the molecules it shares, and
\emph{admit} the calibration iff it both correlates ($|R|\ge R_{\min}$) and passes the noise gate
($\mathrm{RMS}/L\le\eta$). An admitted unit converts all of its rows, whose per-molecule medians
join the anchor set.

We run this as an outer \emph{fixed-point} iteration (co-training): after each admission pass we
reconcile all observations to a per-molecule consensus, feed that cleaner, larger anchor set back,
and \emph{anneal} the gate one notch looser --- from $(\eta,R_{\min})=(0.25,0.90)$ to
$(0.65,0.78)$ --- so the high-confidence backbone is laid down before the marginal tail. The
iteration continues until a pass at the loosest gate admits nothing. On the corrected switchboard
it converges in six outer steps, admitting \num{19} units and reaching \num{135} further molecules
(\num{2675}$\to$\num{2810}); the held-out FreeSolv agreement holds flat at $\approx$\SI{0.32}{\kcal}
as coverage grows --- a clean coverage/accuracy Pareto with a self-terminating stop (FreeSolv is
never an anchor). (Before the logarithmic-unit fix the loop had to \emph{rescue} those spellings
itself and admitted \num{26} units; once the switchboard converts them directly its remaining job
is smaller, which is the desired outcome.) The loop is self-validating: it independently recovers the
air/water sign of the dimensionless Henry constant ($\log(M/M)$ fit with slope $-0.99$ under the
gas/water process and $+1.00$ under water/gas) and cracks opaque strings a human would struggle
with, e.g.\ \texttt{MPv/(RTCw)} ($\ln$, $R=0.99$) and \texttt{logK=y/x at 1 atm} ($R=0.99$).

\subsection{From regex switchboard to a declarative rule engine}
\label{sec:rules}
The hand-written switchboard grew brittle --- the two conversion bugs above were both parsing
faults, not physics errors. We therefore reimplemented conversion as a small \emph{dimensional-
analysis rule engine} (\texttt{unit\_rules.py}): a unit string is de-wrapped of any
$\log/\ln/{-}\log$ prefix, tokenised into a product/quotient of $\langle$prefix$\rangle\langle$base$\rangle^{p}$
factors, reduced to a net dimension signature (pressure, amount, volume, mass, mole-fraction,
energy), and \emph{classified} into a physical quantity (vapour pressure, concentration,
mole-fraction, the four Henry forms, free energy, or a dimensionless ratio) that is then converted.
Adding a unit is a one-line table entry, never a new branch. The engine converts \num{65} distinct
(unit,\,process) combinations versus \num{57} for the switchboard, resolves the mass-fraction /
Henry-ratio ambiguity by process (a same-dimension ratio under AQSOL is a solubility fraction, under
KWG/KGW a Henry ratio), and \emph{improves} FreeSolv agreement to \SI{0.275}{\kcal} ($R=0.972$). The
handful of strings it still cannot infer (\texttt{MPv/(RTCw)}, \texttt{logK=y/x at 1 atm}) are exactly
the exotic named expressions the active-learning loop calibrates --- a clean division of labour
between deterministic rules and data-driven fallback.

\subsection{Guthrie's own metadata: a third estimate and a trust flag}
\label{sec:meta}
The original Excel carries columns the flat CSV drops: \texttt{final} (Guthrie's \emph{own}
converted $\dGhyd$, \num{737} rows), \texttt{error1} (a per-measurement \emph{trust flag} --- the
value \SI{1.93}{\kcal}, and its kJ twin \num{8.08}, mark data he judged untrustworthy), and pH
conditions. Two findings follow. First, on the \num{176} molecules where Guthrie hand-computed a
$\dGhyd$ that also appears in FreeSolv, our automated rule-engine-plus-median consensus is
\emph{closer to FreeSolv} than his own values (MAE \SI{0.153}{} vs \SI{0.267}{\kcal}; $R=0.988$ vs
$0.943$): pooling the redundant measurements beats a single curated number. Second, using his trust
flag as a hard filter \emph{hurts} --- dropping the \num{2884} flagged observations worsens FreeSolv
agreement from \SI{0.195}{} to \SI{0.240}{\kcal} --- so it belongs as a soft observation weight in
the hierarchical model, not a cut.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Homoset: a proportional-similarity consensus gate}
The Homoset (``homogeneous set'') method is not new: it was developed in the 1970s--1980s to
standardize scattered literature values of \emph{human olfactory detection thresholds}, and is
the procedure behind the harmonized inventory of Devos, Patte, Rouault, Laffort and Van
Gemert~\cite{devos1990}. Faced with the same problem we have here --- many disagreeing
measurements of one quantity per compound, from heterogeneous sources --- it builds a
``homogeneous set'' by aligning sources with a median offset and admitting them only when the
relative scatter of the set is small enough. The method has exactly \emph{two parameters}: a
\textbf{noise level} $\eta$ (the fraction of the amplitude scale that scatter may occupy) and
the \textbf{dimension value} $L$ (the amplitude scale itself). We apply this classical
odour-threshold machinery, unchanged, to hydration free energies. It treats each measurement
type as a data \emph{source} and the direct free-energy records as the trusted \emph{reference},
and proceeds in two stages.

\paragraph{The homogeneity test.} A set of values $x_1,\dots,x_n$ is a \emph{homogeneous set}
when its root-mean-square scatter about its median is at most the noise level $\eta$ times the
dimension $L$:
\begin{equation}
\delta = \operatorname{median}(x_i), \quad
\mathrm{RMS} = \sqrt{\tfrac{1}{n}\textstyle\sum_i (x_i-\delta)^2}, \quad
\text{homogeneous} \iff \underbrace{\frac{\mathrm{RMS}}{L}}_{\text{noise}} \le \eta
\;\Longleftrightarrow\; \mathrm{PS} \equiv 1-\frac{\mathrm{RMS}}{L} \ge 1-\eta .
\label{eq:ps}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathrm{PS}$ is the proportional-similarity index of the original method; setting the
acceptance threshold by a sample-size-adjusted $\chi^2$ rule, $1-\eta = 1-\sqrt{\chi^2_{n-1}(\alpha)/3n}$,
recovers the statistical form, but the two operating parameters are $\eta$ and $L$.

\paragraph{Stage 1 --- source alignment.} For a non-reference source $s$, the differences
$d_i$ to the reference over their overlap are tested by Eq.~\eqref{eq:ps}. If homogeneous, $s$
is admitted and shifted by $-\delta_s$; otherwise it is discarded. We sweep the two parameters:
$L$ over a \emph{data-adaptive} scale $L=\max((\max d-\min d)/2,\,0.5)$ and a \emph{fixed
physical} scale $L=\SI{0.6}{\kcal}$ (FreeSolv's typical experimental uncertainty), and the noise
level over $\eta\in\{0.25,\,0.50\}$.

\paragraph{Stage 2 --- consensus and triage.} For each molecule the consensus is the median
over admitted, aligned observations; the molecule is flagged \emph{conflicted} when its own
observations fail the homogeneity test (Eq.~\eqref{eq:ps}) against the physical dimension
$L=\SI{0.6}{\kcal}$, yielding a direct \texttt{use\_for\_training} decision.

\subsection{mixle: hierarchical partial pooling}
We reproduce, in NumPy, the hierarchical partial-pooling construction of the
\texttt{mixle} probabilistic-modelling library~\cite{mixle}, whose compositional dialect
expresses a random-effects prior directly as \texttt{Normal(Normal(mu0, tau), sigma)}. The
model is generative. With $y_{ij}$ the $j$-th observation of molecule $i$ from
source $s(ij)$,
\begin{equation}
y_{ij} \mid \mu_i \sim \mathcal N\!\big(\mu_i + b_{s(ij)},\, \sigma_{s(ij)}^2\big), \qquad
\mu_i \sim \mathcal N(\mu_0, \tau^2), \qquad b_{\mathrm{ref}} = 0 .
\label{eq:mixle}
\end{equation}
Anchoring the reference bias to zero fixes the absolute scale to the trusted free-energy
source. The per-source bias $b_s$, per-source noise $\sigma_s$, prior mean $\mu_0$, between-molecule
variance $\tau^2$, and the shrunken posteriors $\mu_i$ are estimated jointly by
expectation--maximization; the posterior mean of $\mu_i$ is a precision-weighted compromise
between its own (bias-corrected) observations and the global prior, so sparsely-measured
molecules are pooled toward $\mu_0$. A robust variant replaces the Gaussian observation model
with a Student-$t$ ($\nu=4$), realized as a scale mixture whose latent per-observation weights
down-weight outliers. Unlike the Homoset gate, mixle never rejects a source: it estimates the
source's systematic offset and \emph{propagates that correction to every molecule}, including
those with no reference measurement.

\paragraph{The two parameters govern the Homoset gate.} The per-source noise
$\mathrm{RMS}_s/L$ makes the gate's behaviour explicit (Table~\ref{tab:gate}). With the
data-adaptive $L$ the three converted sources are all tight (noise $0.09$--$0.16$), so any tested
noise level admits them all and the consensus barely improves on a plain median. With the fixed
physical $L=\SI{0.6}{\kcal}$ the noise is $0.9$--$2.7$, far above any $\eta$, so all converted
sources are rejected and only the direct free-energy records survive: the gate becomes a hard
source-quality filter, and this strict setting attains the best median-based accuracy. The
least-trusted converted source --- the vapour-pressure/solubility pairing, with the largest gate
noise --- is exactly the one the hierarchical model assigns the largest bias (\SI{+0.91}{\kcal},
below), an independent corroboration of which source is least reliable.

\paragraph{mixle corrects rather than rejects.} The Gaussian model estimates modest systematic
source biases of \SI{+0.91}{\kcal} for the vapour-pressure/solubility pairing and
$\approx$\SI{+0.2}{\kcal} for the two Henry sources (relative to the free-energy reference), and
subtracts them. This yields the lowest overall MAE and RMSE, and --- because the bias is shared
across molecules --- it is the only method that improves sparse molecules that cannot be aligned by
the gate. For example, a two-source molecule (InChIKey \texttt{XOGPDSATLSAZEK}) has FreeSolv value
\SI{-11.53}{\kcal}; the raw median and the Homoset consensus sit at \SI{-6.77}{\kcal} (the two
sources disagree and there is nothing to align against), whereas mixle-robust recovers
\SI{-11.48}{\kcal} by trusting the lower-bias source. On the \num{286} molecules whose replicates
fail the homogeneity test, mixle-robust reaches an MAE of \SI{0.276}{\kcal} versus
\SI{0.314}{\kcal} for the strict Homoset gate and \SI{0.365}{\kcal} for the raw median.

\section{Discussion}
The two methods embody a general trade-off in multi-source curation. The Homoset gate makes
discrete, auditable decisions --- accept or reject each source, flag or keep each molecule --- and
so produces exactly the artifact a curator wants: a defensible \texttt{use\_for\_training} label
per molecule. Its accuracy, however, is entirely contingent on choosing the noise scale $L$ to
match the true measurement error; with a data-adaptive $L$ it is scarcely better than a median.
The hierarchical model makes continuous, joint decisions --- soft per-source bias correction and
soft per-observation down-weighting --- and so extracts more signal, keeps all data, needs no
threshold, and generalizes source corrections to unmeasured cases; but it emits no explicit
curation flag and is less transparent. A practical pipeline uses both: reconcile values with the
hierarchical model, then triage with the Homoset gate to decide which molecules a human should
re-examine.

A caveat on evaluation: the FreeSolv overlap is enriched in molecules that possess a clean
free-energy record, which favours the strict Homoset setting (it can always fall back to that
record). On the roughly \num{1900} non-overlap molecules whose only data are Henry or pairing
measurements, the hierarchical model's ability to transfer a learned source bias should be more
valuable. Extending an independent validation to that regime is the natural next step, as is
modelling temperature and standard-state as explicit effects rather than pre-correcting them.

\section{Conclusion}
We converted the heterogeneous GuthrieSolv dump into \num{16357} standardized hydration
free-energy observations and used its redundancy to compare a proportional-similarity consensus
gate with a hierarchical partial-pooling model. Both recover the signal that naïve pooling
misses. The gate is the better curator under a physically-anchored noise scale; the hierarchical
model is the better reconciler and the more robust on hard, reference-poor molecules. The
harmonized dataset, conversion code, and both estimators are released to support continued
community curation of the database.
